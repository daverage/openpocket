# Acoustic strings and brass for guitar

The current BRASS and STRINGS modes reshape the guitar waveform. To get an acoustic instrument sound, use the guitar as a controller for an independent voice. Prototype a **cello-range bowed voice and solo trumpet** first. A cello starts at C2, so it covers the guitar's low E at its original pitch, while a violin starts at G3. Give trumpet an octave offset so the guitarist can drive samples inside their intended register. Keep chord detection as a separate experiment so recognition mistakes and instrument timbre can be evaluated independently. [Yamaha cello and violin ranges](https://hub.europe.yamaha.com/articles/the-essential-cello) · [Current voicing research](SYNTH-VOICING-RESEARCH.md) · [Current engine](../engine/px_dsp.c)

**Host prototype results:** [REALISTIC-INSTRUMENT-PROTOTYPE.md](REALISTIC-INSTRUMENT-PROTOTYPE.md) (`tools/sim/acoustic_voice.sh`).

## Instrument voice options

| Method | Best use | Important trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loopable multisamples | Fastest route to recognizable bow scrape, tongue transients, body resonance and a consistent instrument identity. | Each attack repeats unless alternate articulations are recorded. Large transpositions change apparent body size and attack speed. Continuous expression needs dynamics crossfades or separate filtering. [SFZ sustain loop](https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/loop_mode/) · [Loop crossfade](https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/loop_crossfade/) |
| Digital waveguide | A solo voice where guitar envelope or expression can control bow velocity and pressure or blowing pressure continuously. | Requires tuning delay length, loss, body or bell response, and stable startup. A physically motivated model is a starting point for voicing. [STK Bowed](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/master/src/Bowed.cpp) · [STK Brass](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/master/src/Brass.cpp) |
| Interpolated wavetables and attack samples | A compact midpoint if sample storage becomes limiting, particularly for brass. Extract phase-aligned sustain cycles at several dynamics and registers, interpolate them against the envelope, and overlay a brief recorded articulation or filtered noise. | Pitch and loudness influence spectral balance. Plain interpolation of periodic waves loses important noisy and inharmonic attack detail. [Derenyi and Dannenberg trumpet synthesis](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rbd/papers/csis98/csis98.htm) · [Serra and Smith spectral modeling](https://mtg.upf.edu/node/251) |

An open source prototype can start with the raw WAVs from Versilian Studios' VSCO 2 Community Edition, which the publisher distributes under CC0. The repository has trumpet, solo violin and cello-section recordings. Use the cello section for a low string-pad comparison and an independently recorded dry solo cello if the target is solo realism. Keep sample provenance, tuning and loop metadata. STK's permissive code license is separate from the sample license. Consult its notices before porting model code. [VSCO 2 CE](https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/) · [VSCO 2 CE source and license](https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE) · [STK license](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/master/LICENSE)

## From guitar to instrument

- **Pitch and gate.** Test the current monophonic HQ tracker as a baseline. Trigger once when pitch confidence is stable, then follow continuous pitch for bends, slides, and vibrato. Keep a stable note identity through small pitch changes, use onset strength to distinguish re-picks from slurs, and use hysteresis on pickup level to release cleanly. Benchmark a YIN-style estimator if period detection flips octaves on quiet or harmonic-rich notes. At 82 Hz, two periods alone take about 24 ms, so measure detection and audible onset separately. [OpenPocket engine](../engine/px_dsp.c) · [YIN paper](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/e6820/papers/deChevK02-yin.pdf)
- **Expression.** Let pick strength set articulation and initial dynamics. Use the smoothed guitar envelope for breath or bow pressure with a slower fall than the plucked string. Use dedicated wet envelopes to keep the pick transient out of the instrument sound. A trumpet stab starts with a short tongue or breath transient and brightens with pressure. A bowed voice ramps in through bow noise, then sustains and releases. A direct attack can be optional for a tight mixed sound. [Trumpet performance synthesis](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rbd/papers/csis98/csis98.htm) · [STK Bowed controls](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/master/src/Bowed.cpp)
- **Chord support.** One mixed pickup channel contains overlapping harmonics. Rendering several samples is straightforward once independent note voices exist. Stable note identities, onsets, releases and bends are the challenge. Start with two-note double stops and compare harmonic-salience candidates against annotated GuitarSet mono mixes. The individual-string recordings provide a reference for what was played. MIDI or separate-string channels can feed the same voice engine with independent note events when those inputs are available. [Klapuri multi-pitch estimator](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1416740) · [GuitarSet](https://zenodo.org/records/3371780) · [Basic Pitch paper for an offline comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)

## Pedal-sized prototype

1. On the host, render the current BRASS and STRINGS using clean DI as the baseline. Render the sample-loop voice and an optional hybrid or waveguide voice with reference pitch, gate and expression supplied by the test fixture, before connecting a tracker. Compare both original and level-matched wet outputs. Start with three root notes and two dynamics per instrument. Use 22.05 kHz mono, keep the attack and a stable crossfaded sustain loop, and fade or play a short tail at release. Store each root's measured pitch, sample positions, and level. Tune the bowed and tongued attacks separately.
2. Drive the preferred voice with the existing single-note HQ tracker and test with clean DI, using quiet and hard picks, slides, sustained vibrato, hammer-ons, fast re-picks and muted releases. Record wrong and missed notes, median and worst pitch-lock and wet-onset times, release time, and level jumps. Use the same phrases on the pedal when the voice is ported. [Bundled DI loops](../assets/loops/ATTRIBUTION.md)
3. Port the preferred voice only after measuring its cost. A bank of two instruments × three roots × two dynamics × 0.5 s at 22.05 kHz is **264,600 bytes** of PCM16, or about **66,150 bytes** at four bits per sample before codec headers and metadata. Both values are planning estimates. Loopable predictive coding would need block checkpoints or a decoded loop cache for repeatable playback across boundaries. Keep samples in flash and use a bounded voice pool. [Firmware memory layout](../firmware/app.ld)
4. Verify the map and worst callback time on both pedals with the feature running alone. The v45 synth-voicing build produced a 372,120-byte app against a 581,564-byte XIP linker region. The nominal 209,444-byte difference is an upper bound subject to section placement and call reach. The build reported only 208 bytes left in the DSP pool, 5,432 bytes in the high RAM code window, and 20,040 bytes in main RAM before stack and runtime considerations. At E2, two float waveguide delay lines each sized to a full period would use roughly 4.2 KiB before filter state, so plan their placement explicitly. The 128-frame audio block is approximately 2.90 ms at 44.1 kHz. These are resource estimates and link results rather than measurements of an acoustic prototype on the device.
